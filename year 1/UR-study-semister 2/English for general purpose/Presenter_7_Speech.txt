
=====================================
   PRESENTER 7 — THE GIG ECONOMY
   Phase 4 — 4 Minutes
=====================================


----- OPENING -----

Thank you.

My name is [YOUR NAME],
and I am here to talk about
Phase 4 — the gig economy.


----- QUESTION TO AUDIENCE -----

Let me ask you something.

When you finish university,
what do you picture happening next?

Most of us picture the same thing.

We graduate,
we send some applications,
and within a few months —
we have a stable job.
A desk. A salary. A routine.

That is a nice picture.

But today, for most young graduates,
that is not what happens.

I am not here to scare you.
I am here to prepare you.


----- THE TRUTH -----

The truth is —

Finding a full-time permanent job
right after graduation
is becoming harder and harder.

Companies are hiring fewer
permanent staff.
Many prefer short contracts.
Many prefer freelancers.

This is called the GIG ECONOMY.


----- WHAT IS GIG WORK? -----

Gig work means you offer your skills
to different clients,
for different projects,
without one fixed employer.

One month — you help a company
design a poster.

Next month — you help someone
write a report.

The month after —
you build a website
for a small business.

You are not one company's employee.

You are your own small business.


----- IMPORTANT MESSAGE -----

Now — when some people hear this,
they feel worried.

They think:
"That sounds unstable.
That sounds like I failed."

I want to stop that thought
right now.

This is NOT failure.

This is the NEW NORMAL.

Millions of professionals
around the world work this way.

The earlier you understand this,
the better prepared you will be.


----- THE STORY OF JEAN -----

[SLOW DOWN HERE]

Let me tell you about Jean.

Jean graduates from university.
He is smart.
He worked hard.
He has his degree.

He starts applying for jobs.

But weeks pass.
Then months.
No full-time offer comes.

Instead of sitting at home waiting,
Jean does something smart.

He goes online and starts offering
graphic design services.

Just 20 US dollars per project.
Small amount. Small start.
But he STARTS.

[PAUSE — 2 seconds]

Six months later —

Jean has 15 regular clients.

He is earning MORE money
than many friends who are still
waiting for the perfect job.

But here is the most important part.

Every project Jean completed —
every poster, every logo,
every design —
became PROOF.

He now has a portfolio.
Real examples. Real results.

So when a company wants to hire
a full-time designer —

Jean is not just a graduate
with a degree.

Jean is a designer
with 15 clients
and 6 months of real experience.

[PAUSE — 2 seconds]

THAT is the power of gig work.

It does not just keep you busy
while you wait.

It makes you BETTER.
It makes you MORE employable.


----- WHERE TO START -----

Now you might be asking —
where do I even start?

There are platforms made for this.

UPWORK and FIVERR —
global websites for small jobs
in writing, design, translation,
marketing, coding, and more.

LINKEDIN SERVICES —
add your skills to your profile
and let professionals find you.

FACEBOOK GROUPS —
local businesses in Rwanda
regularly post looking
for freelancers.

You do not need to wait
until you graduate.

You can create a profile
THIS WEEK.

One small job this semester.
One project. That is enough to start.


----- CLOSING -----

So let me leave you with this.

The world of work has changed.

The question is not whether
the gig economy exists —
it does.

The question is whether
YOU are going to be ready for it.

Do not wait at home
for the perfect job offer.

Start small.
Build your skills.
Build your portfolio.
Treat yourself like a small business —

because in today's world,
that is exactly what you are.


----- HANDOVER -----

I will now hand over to my colleague,
who will talk about something
just as important —

protecting your mental health
while you navigate all of this.

Thank you.
